THE PRINCIPLE

Don't contact a customer because a field is blank.

First ask whether the missing activity reflects a real onboarding issue, delayed sync, or an intentionally limited workflow. A human should review context before triggering a play.

Show methodology, rules and limitations

Data is generated locally using a fixed deterministic seed; no legal documents or real accounts are used. Illustrative checks include missing owner, stale CRM update, missing event feed, and contradictory usage signals. Missing events are null, displayed as “Unknown”, and never interpreted as zero. Records with quality exceptions always route to data verification first. Remaining early-stage accounts with no observed use route to onboarding review; accounts aged 35+ days with fewer than three observed events route to a context review for optional guidance; otherwise monitor. None of these rules estimates churn or measures customer value. Segments, thresholds and activity fields are teaching examples only, not Spellbook's actual schema. A CSM should validate intent, sync latency, eligibility and customer context before contacting anyone. Counts use both selected filters. The four outcomes are mutually exclusive; verification + candidate-help reviews + monitoring equals the selected account count. Flags can overlap within a verification case. The gate means only “no modeled exception”, not verified data or known account health. Age and event thresholds are analyst-defined examples, not Spellbook policy.

FROM A FLAG TO A RESPONSIBLE NEXT STEP

Different evidence.
Different next move.

A hypothetical low-activity record is a starting point. The first owner should depend on the uncertainty—not on how quickly a message can be sent.

01 / SOURCE UNCERTAIN

Verify the record.

Data or Ops checks ownership, sync freshness, account joins and seat definitions. Product checks event coverage.

02 / CONTEXT NEEDED

Review the customer journey.

CS confirms eligibility, workflow and intent before deciding whether guidance would be welcome.

03 / NO MODELED EXCEPTION

Monitor, without a health claim.

Keep the account in its normal workflow. Passing a small rule set does not prove that a customer is successful.

What I'd need to validate with the team

Approved anonymized account mapping, ownership, an event dictionary, feed freshness, lifecycle timestamps, provisioned seats and entitlements, customer context, and privacy boundaries. Renewal context only if appropriate and permitted. No client documents are needed for this public demonstration.

03 / A PRACTICAL 30-DAY PILOT

Measure decisions,
not dashboard clicks.

Test whether the reliability check improves the usefulness of a small review queue compared with existing practice. No improvement is assumed.

WEEK 01

Agree on definitions

With CS, Product and Data, establish what counts as first value, a stale record, missing ownership and valid activity. Check data collection and consent boundaries.

WEEK 02

Audit a sample

Have people inspect 30–50 cases, record false alerts, segment exceptions and cases where absence of activity has a benign explanation.

WEEKS 03–04

Test one helpful play

With human review, compare a narrow onboarding intervention to existing practice. Observe time-to-value, actionable-alert rate, support effort and unwanted contacts.

What would change the recommendation?

If signals are too incomplete, delayed or misleading, improve instrumentation and account matching first. If customers don't find the suggested outreach useful, change the play rather than optimizing the number of messages sent.
